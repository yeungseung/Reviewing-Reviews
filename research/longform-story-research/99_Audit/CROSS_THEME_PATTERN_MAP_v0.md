> Status: PROVISIONAL AUDIT ARTIFACT  
> Purpose: reorganize the existing 165 completed Themes around recurring mechanisms rather than treating the original 86 top-level items as fixed truth.

## 0. Interpretation rules

This map does **not** treat repeated Gemini claims as independent confirmation.

For every candidate mechanism distinguish:

1. Theme recurrence — how many separately researched Themes rediscovered the idea
2. Source-record recurrence — how many SRC records mention it
3. Independent original-source families — deduplicated papers/books/research lines
4. External verification status — whether the underlying publication and core qualitative claim have been checked
5. Domain-transfer distance — how far the original evidence is from longform storytelling
6. Quantitative reliability — whether percentages, ratios, timing thresholds, or platform KPIs are actually supported
7. Engine value — whether the mechanism can become a useful decision rule

A repeated source is a convergence signal, not another independent piece of evidence.

# 2. Candidate higher-order mechanisms

These are **mechanism hypotheses for Research Map v2**, not final Story Engine laws.

## M1 — Active Causal Model Construction
Priority: **A — precision research priority**

Working proposition:
> Audiences do not merely receive events; they actively construct a causal and goal-based situation model. Engagement can increase when new information helps build, revise, or complete that model.

Recurring evidence families:
- Graesser, Singer & Trabasso
- Trabasso causal-chain research
- situation-model / event-indexing research
- QUEST question-answering research

Original categories touched include:
- basic immersion
- conflict opening
- WHY→THEREFORE
- clue/inference design
- Rehook
- Viewer Relevance
- Peak buildup

Audit warning:
Do not convert this into exact rules such as "leave 20% unexplained" without direct evidence.

Engine relevance:
Very high. This may become one of the central cognitive mechanisms of the engine.

---

## M2 — Event / State Updating
Priority: **A — precision research priority**

Working proposition:
> Perceived changes in goals, causes, locations, agents, relationships, risk, or other event dimensions can trigger situation-model updating and event segmentation.

Recurring evidence families:
- Zacks et al. event perception
- Zwaan event-indexing
- Kurby & Zacks event segmentation
- related memory-boundary work

External verification supports the qualitative mechanism.

Original categories touched:
- #1 emotion/state change
- #24 Causal Spine
- #29 Prediction Engine
- #45 Story State

Audit warning:
Current 2.3x / 2.4x / 79% / 64% Story Engine claims are not established by the verified literature.

Engine relevance:
Very high. This may be a better parent mechanism for several existing categories.

---

## M3 — Appraisal-Driven Emotion Update
Priority: **A — precision research priority**

Working proposition:
> Emotional response changes when the audience's appraisal of agency, goal relevance, probability, controllability/coping potential, legitimacy, or situational meaning changes.

Recurring research:
- Roseman
- Scherer
- Oatley & Johnson-Laird
- Affect Infusion / appraisal-related work

External verification of Roseman (1991):
- N=120 college students
- five appraisal dimensions manipulated
- appraisals significantly affected emotional intensity
- predictions were stronger for some dimensions than others

Audit warning:
This supports appraisal→emotion relationships, not "87% emotion conversion" or exact longform timing rules.

Engine relevance:
Very high. This could replace vague "change the emotion" rules with a state/appraisal transformation model.

---

## M4 — Capacity-Limited Message Processing
Priority: **A — precision research priority**

Working proposition:
> Attention, encoding, storage, and retrieval operate under capacity constraints, so information density, pacing, modality competition, and emotional salience can change what is processed and remembered.

Recurring evidence families:
- Lang LCMP/LC4MP
- Sweller cognitive load
- multimedia-learning work

External verification:
- Lang's original model explicitly addresses mediated-message processing
- a later meta-analysis covering 142 articles / 683 effects reported pooled effects across cognitive load, motivation, and memory domains around r=.314–.398

Engine relevance:
Very high for pacing, data density, subtitles, visual complexity, and information/emotion sequencing.

Audit warning:
This still does not establish universal "X seconds per cut" or exact content-density ratios.

---

## M5 — Curiosity as Managed Uncertainty / Information Gap
Priority: **B+ — strong candidate, precision research needed**

Working proposition:
> Curiosity can emerge when the audience recognizes a meaningful, potentially resolvable gap between what is known and what is wanted.

Recurring evidence families:
- Loewenstein curiosity
- Berlyne conflict/arousal/curiosity
- need-for-closure work
- suspense research
- open-loop / question-answering research

Original categories touched:
- Opening
- Hook
- Open Loop
- Rehook
- Chapter
- Payoff

Research-map implication:
Hook → Open Loop → Chapter → Payoff may be stages of an uncertainty-management cycle rather than four independent top-level mechanisms.

Audit warning:
Exact loop counts, timing intervals, and dopamine claims are currently low-confidence.

---

## M6 — Habituation / Sensitization / Contrast
Priority: **B — promising with domain-transfer caution**

Working proposition:
> Repeated stimulation can reduce responding through habituation, while stimulus intensity/context can produce sensitization; novelty or contrast can alter responding.

External verification:
Groves & Thompson (1970) is a real dual-process habituation theory. Later reviews continue to support habituation/sensitization as broad learning phenomena.

Important limitation:
Much of the foundational literature is animal, reflex, infant, or psychophysiological research.

Engine relevance:
Potentially useful for emotional repetition, visual repetition, escalation fatigue, and contrast.

Audit warning:
"same emotion for N minutes causes X% drop" is not justified by this evidence.

---

## M7 — Prediction / Expectation Updating
Priority: **B — important but theoretically mixed**

Working proposition:
> Audience engagement may involve continuous prediction, expectation confirmation/violation, and model updating.

Recurring families:
- event-prediction work
- reward-prediction-error research
- predictive-processing/free-energy theory
- suspense research

Original categories:
- Prediction Engine
- prediction success/failure as reward
- Rehook Timing
- Story State

Audit issue:
Some current sources are very far from narrative viewing (neural reward learning, theoretical predictive coding).

Research need:
Separate:
1. perceptual prediction
2. reward prediction
3. narrative expectation
4. explicit audience guessing

Do not treat them as one established mechanism until this distinction is made.

---

## M8 — Payoff / Closure as Uncertainty Resolution
Priority: **B**

Working proposition:
> Resolution value may depend on what uncertainty, causal gap, goal, or expectation is closed—not simply on emotional intensity at the end.

Recurring families:
- need for closure
- satisfaction/expectation-disconfirmation
- suspense resolution
- peak/end and memory research

Research-map implication:
#20 Hook, #47 Chapter Block, #51 Payoff and parts of #52 Ending Emotion may belong to one lifecycle:
**Open → Maintain → Transform → Resolve → Re-evaluate**

Audit warning:
Peak-End findings should not be universalized from short aversive experiences to all longform evaluation without direct evidence.

## M9 — Trust / Provenance / Correction
Priority: **B+ and strategically important**

Recurring source family:

Only #59 T1/T2 are researched; #59 T3 and #60–#63 remain incomplete/unresearched.

Audit implication:
This area should move **up**, because weaknesses in this mechanism are also weaknesses in the current research pipeline itself.

## M10 — Viewer Relevance / Human Scale
Priority: **B**

Recurring families:

- relevance theory

Audit caution:
"humanization" can bias judgment and should not be treated as a universally desirable emotional intensifier.

# 6. Current design hypothesis for the Story Engine

The first 59 research items increasingly suggest that many v2 rules may reduce to a smaller dynamic system:

```
FACT / EVENT
    ↓
CAUSAL + GOAL MODEL
    ↓
PREDICTION / QUESTION
    ↓
NEW INFORMATION
    ↓
STATE / MEANING UPDATE
    ↓
APPRAISAL UPDATE
    ↓
EMOTION / JUDGMENT UPDATE
    ↓
NEW QUESTION / NEW EXPECTATION
    ↓
PAYOFF / CLOSURE / REFRAME
```

Processing capacity and trust operate as cross-cutting constraints:

```
CAPACITY / ATTENTION  ─────────────┐
                                  ↓
FACT → MODEL → PREDICTION → UPDATE → PAYOFF
                                  ↑
TRUST / SOURCE / PROVENANCE ──────┘
```

This is only a **candidate architecture**.

The next audit task is to test whether the apparent convergence is genuine across independent source families or is an artifact of repeated reuse of the same small set of papers.

## Important result

The cross-theme convergence is **not entirely an artifact of duplicate SRC records**.

For M1, external checks confirm multiple genuinely different research lines:
- Graesser, Singer & Trabasso (1994): constructionist inference / search-after-meaning
- Trabasso & van den Broek (1985): causal-network centrality and narrative memory/importance
- Zwaan, Langston & Graesser (1995): five-dimensional event-indexing situation model

However, the audit also confirms that source-record quality can be poor even inside a valid higher-order pattern.

Therefore Pattern Map v2 must score:
1. mechanism convergence
2. individual source-record integrity

separately.
