# PAYOFF & ADAPTATION TAXONOMY v0

> Audit artifact  
> Goal: prevent Story Engine rules from collapsing adaptation, closure, satisfaction, retrospective evaluation, and ending emotion into one mechanism.

# 1. Adaptation side

## A1 — Habituation / Sensitization
Origin:
behavioral and neurophysiological learning research.

Useful proposition:
repetition can reduce responding, while strong/contextual stimulation can produce sensitization.

Do not infer:
- longform timing constants
- exact emotional half-life
- mandatory "valley"
- receptor-reset language

## A2 — Hedonic Adaptation
Origin:
well-being / hedonic response literature.

Useful proposition:
subjective affective impact of continuing circumstances often attenuates over time, with important limits and moderators.

Do not infer:
- 8–10 minute media-emotion clock
- amygdala/limbic reset
- immediate within-video tone-switch requirement

## A3 — Media-specific adaptation
Status:
RESEARCH GAP.

This is the bridge the current corpus mostly assumes rather than demonstrates.

Future research should prioritize:
- continuous narrative exposure
- repeated emotional cueing
- audiovisual arousal
- within-session fatigue/decline
- recovery/contrast periods

# 2. Payoff side

## P-A — Narrative Closure
Definition:
feeling of finality associated with resolution of salient narrative questions.

Evidence type:
primarily narrative/aesthetic theory in current corpus.

Engine use:
track which questions are salient and whether they are answered, transformed, superseded, or intentionally left open.

## P-B — Promise / Expectation Fulfillment
Definition:
comparison between prior expectation/promise and experienced outcome.

Current evidence:
consumer expectation-disconfirmation offers an analogy, but narrative/platform evidence must be researched directly.

Engine use:
connect packaging/opening promise to eventual delivery.

## P-C — Retrospective Evaluation
Definition:
post-experience evaluation may weight some moments disproportionately.

Peak/End research is relevant here.

Engine use:
flag endings and peaks as potentially high-leverage moments.

Do not use:
universal formula or guaranteed recommendation/rewatch effects.

## P-D — Emotional Resolution
Definition:
what emotional state or emotional complexity remains at the end.

Research needs:
- genre differences
- bittersweet vs positive vs negative
- eudaimonic vs hedonic appreciation
- open endings
- ambiguity tolerance

## P-E — Epistemic Resolution
Definition:
answering, reframing, or clarifying the central question/model.

Likely overlaps M5 curiosity/uncertainty and M1 causal-model construction.

## P-F — Moral / Value Resolution
Definition:
justice, responsibility, admiration, warning, moral re-evaluation.

Likely overlaps appraisal, moral emotion, and causal attribution.

## P-G — Actionable Resolution
Definition:
decision rule, recommendation, behavior implication.

Important for review/information content but distinct from emotional closure.

# 3. Reclassifying the current nine payoff types

Current taxonomy:
- Answer
- Justice
- Catharsis
- Reframe
- Admiration
- Warning
- Decision Rule
- Hope
- Bittersweet

Audit view:
These may be useful **surface outputs**, but they mix different dimensions.

Examples:
- Answer = epistemic function
- Justice = moral outcome
- Catharsis = emotional process/outcome
- Reframe = cognitive transformation
- Admiration = emotional/moral response
- Warning = communicative function
- Decision Rule = practical function
- Hope = emotional state
- Bittersweet = mixed valence

Therefore mutual exclusivity is structurally unlikely.

Research Map v2 should consider a multidimensional payoff representation rather than nine flat boxes.

Possible dimensions:
1. epistemic closure
2. causal/model update
3. moral resolution
4. emotional valence/arousal/complexity
5. actionability
6. future orientation
7. ambiguity/open-endedness
8. promise fulfillment

A single ending could score on multiple dimensions.

---

# 4. Candidate Story Engine ending state

Instead of asking:
> Which one of nine payoff types is this?

Ask:
- What active question/promise is being resolved or transformed?
- What belief/model should be different?
- What emotional state remains?
- What moral/value judgment remains?
- What action/decision implication remains?
- What uncertainty is intentionally left open?
- Is the ending faithful to the evidence and story promise?

This representation is more compatible with a future engine and avoids forcing research findings into arbitrary categorical boxes.
